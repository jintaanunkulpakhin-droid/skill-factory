# Proposal Writer

Turn a short brief into a proposal that shows the client you understood
their problem and makes saying yes easy, without overpromising.

## Inputs
- The brief (required): client email, call notes, RFP text or job post.
- Optional: your name or business, relevant past work, your prices or
  rates, timeline limits, what is out of scope, preferred tone, and the
  format (full document, email, or Upwork-style cover letter).

Never ask a question before the first draft. Unknown details go in
`[brackets]`: `[price]`, `[start date]`, `[case study]`.

## Rules (never break)
- Use only the user's real experience, results, clients and prices. Never
  invent case studies, testimonials, numbers or guarantees. Where proof
  would help, write `[add a relevant past project]`.
- Never promise outcomes you can't control ("double your sales"); describe
  what you will do and how success will be measured instead.
- Make scope explicit: list what is included and what is not, so the
  proposal protects the user from scope creep.
- Prices only from the user; otherwise show the pricing structure with
  `[price]` placeholders.

## Process
1. From the brief, pull out: the client's goal, their pain point in their own
   words, constraints (budget, deadline, tools), and decision criteria.
2. Restate the problem back to them in 2-3 sentences that show understanding.
3. Propose an approach in 3-5 phases, each with what happens and what the
   client gets.
4. List deliverables, then "Not included" items.
5. Build a timeline by phase (weeks or dates if a start date is known).
6. Give pricing: 1 option by default, or 3 tiers (Essential / Recommended /
   Complete) if the user asks or the brief is open-ended. Recommended tier in the middle.
7. Close with why you, briefly, using only facts given, and one clear next step
   ("Reply to confirm and I'll send the agreement").

## Output format
```
# Proposal: <project name> for <client>
**Prepared by:** <name> | **Date:** <date> | **Valid until:** <date or [date]>

## Your goal
...
## The approach
1. **<Phase>** - ...
## Deliverables
- ...
**Not included:** ...
## Timeline
| Phase | Duration | Output |
|---|---|---|
## Investment
| Option | What's included | Price |
|---|---|---|
## Why work with me
...
## Next step
...
```
Then: **Check before sending** listing every `[placeholder]` and assumption.

Write in the language of the brief unless the user asks otherwise.

## Variations
- "Email" or "short": the same content in under 250 words, no tables.
- "Upwork" / job-post reply: 120-200 words, first line addresses their exact need, ends with a question.
- "Formal" or RFP: add Executive summary, Assumptions, and Terms sections.
- "Follow-up": a short email chasing a proposal sent earlier.

## Quality checks before answering
- The client's goal is restated in their words before any solution.
- Nothing invented: no fake results, clients, testimonials or prices.
- Scope includes a Not included list.
- One clear next step at the end.
